# 02: Target Personas, Cognitive Archetypes & Pain Points

---

## 1. Demographic & Cultural Context

Designing for **Bharat** requires discarding conventional Western SaaS mental models. Our users operate within unique constraints:
- **Shared Device Culture**: In over 60% of rural and semi-urban households, a single smartphone is shared among 4–6 family members, typically owned by the father or eldest son.
- **High Audio & Video Affinity, Low Text Appetite**: Users who struggle to read complex newspaper articles or official notices consume hours of YouTube, WhatsApp voice notes, and regional reels effortlessly.
- **Severe Bureaucracy Anxiety**: Fear of police, revenue officers (Tehsildar, Patwari), and government clerks is palpable. Citizens assume government forms are traps where minor mistakes lead to legal punishment or welfare cancellation.
- **The "Middleman Norm"**: Citizens do not expect government services to be simple or free. They have been conditioned to believe that paying an agent/broker (*dalal*) is an inevitable tax of existence.

## 2. Deep-Archetype Personas

### Persona 1: Rameshwar "Ramesh" Yadav — The Marginal Farmer
*“I just want to know if my small field gets the yearly fertilizer assistance, without making five bus trips to the Tehsil.”*

- **Demographics**: 52 years old, smallholder farmer (1.4 acres), Village near Gorakhpur, Uttar Pradesh.
- **Languages**: Bhojpuri (Spoken, primary), Hindi (Spoken fluent, reading limited to basic signage and numbers).
- **Digital Literacy**: Owns a budget 4G Android phone (2GB RAM). Uses WhatsApp voice messages, YouTube agricultural tips, and phone calls. Cannot type on a virtual keyboard.
- **Household**: Wife, three children, elderly mother. Total monthly family income: ₹8,500 – ₹12,000 (seasonal).
- **Primary Need**: Wants to enroll in **PM-KISAN** (₹6,000 annual installment) and obtain a **Kisan Credit Card (KCC)** for crop loan subsidy.
- **Core Friction & Pain Points**:
  - Unsure whether his land records (*Khatauni*) are linked to his Aadhaar.
  - Visited a local cyber café twice; the operator charged him ₹250 each time and told him "the server is down, come next week."
  - Cannot read the official English/High-Hindi portal error messages.
- **Mental Model**: Thinks in terms of crops, seasons (*Kharif, Rabi*), and land measurement units (*Bigha, Kattha*), not hectares or ministry departments.

### Persona 2: Lakshmi Venkatesh — The Urban Informal Earner & Mother
*“Every rupee matters for my daughter's future. Tell me plainly what papers I need so I don't lose my day's wages standing in the wrong queue.”*

- **Demographics**: 36 years old, domestic cook and housekeeper in Bengaluru, Karnataka (migrated from rural Tamil Nadu border).
- **Languages**: Tamil (Mother tongue), Kannada (Spoken street fluency), cannot read written Kannada or English.
- **Digital Literacy**: Uses an older smartphone handed down by her employer. Scans QR codes for UPI payments at grocery stores (*PhonePe/GPay audio chimes*). Does not use web browsers.
- **Household**: Single mother of two children (8-year-old daughter, 13-year-old son). Monthly income: ₹15,000.
- **Primary Need**: Seeks maternity benefit entitlements for her sister (**PMMVY**), health cover under **Ayushman Bharat (PM-JAY)**, and long-term savings under **Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana** for her daughter.
- **Core Friction & Pain Points**:
  - Missing a day of work to visit a government office costs her ₹500 in daily wages.
  - Confused about which identity documents are valid—her Aadhaar card still has her village address, while her gas bill has her rental address in Bengaluru.
  - Intimidated by clerical questions regarding "SECC 2011 Deprivation Category" or "BPL Card verification".
- **Mental Model**: Action-oriented, visual learner. Responds immediately to audio instructions in conversational colloquial Tamil/Kannada.

### Persona 3: Arindam Mondal — The Rural Weaver & Micro-Entrepreneur
*“I make handloom sarees that sell for ₹3,000 in Kolkata, but I need ₹20,000 working capital to buy thread. The bank says apply online, but the form asks for things I don't understand.”*

- **Demographics**: 28 years old, master artisan and handloom weaver, Nadia District, West Bengal.
- **Languages**: Bengali (Fluent spoken and written), broken Hindi, negligible English.
- **Digital Literacy**: Comfortable with Facebook Marketplace, WhatsApp product catalogs, and basic digital payments. Can type in Bengali script using phonetic keyboard.
- **Household**: Self, wife, artisan brother. Monthly revenue: ₹22,000 (erratic cash flows).
- **Primary Need**: Seeking collateral-free working capital loan through **PM SVANidhi** or **MUDRA Yojana (Shishu/Kishore category)**.
- **Core Friction & Pain Points**:
  - Does not possess audited profit & loss statements or formal GST registration; operates via artisan trade register and bank statements.
  - The bank manager refuses to explain the scheme guidelines and demands "printouts of online approval".
  - Online government portals require uploading documents compressed to "<50KB PDF", which he has no technical capacity to generate on his phone.
- **Mental Model**: Entrepreneurial, pride in craftsmanship, desires respect and clear business-friendly language rather than feeling like a charity beggar.

### Persona 4: Sunita Rathod — The First-Generation Student & Family "Tech Proxy"
*“My parents can't read the letters that arrive from the government. I have to apply for my college scholarship and also fix my father's ration card.”*

- **Demographics**: 19 years old, 2nd-year B.Sc. student at a government college, Latur, Maharashtra.
- **Languages**: Marathi (Native), Hindi (Fluent), English (Intermediate school-level reading).
- **Digital Literacy**: Smartphone native. Uses Instagram, Telegram study groups, Google Search, and college portals. Acts as the designated **"Digital Proxy"** for her extended family and village neighbors.
- **Household**: Father (wage laborer), Mother (anganwadi worker), younger brother. Monthly family income: ₹11,000.
- **Primary Need**: Applying for **Post-Matric Scholarship for OBC Students (MahaDBT / National Scholarship Portal)** and adding her brother's name to the **Ration Card (NFSA)**.
- **Core Friction & Pain Points**:
  - Faces sudden scholarship portal deadlines with zero notification when documents are flagged for re-upload.
  - Struggling to navigate conflicting requirements between the college administration and state social welfare department.
  - Overwhelmed by being the sole digital helper for 6 different relatives while balancing her exams.
- **Mental Model**: Methodical, tech-literate, but frustrated by clunky desktop-only government portals designed in the year 2005.
